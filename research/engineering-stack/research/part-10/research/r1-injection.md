## Ledger rows

- **P12-01** (threats include prompt injection, jailbreaking, data exfiltration, toxic output). **Holds: confirmed, with the same caveat.** OWASP LLM01:2026 lists jailbreaking as a subset of prompt injection and data exfiltration as an outcome (R1-06, R1-09); toxic or harmful content appears in OWASP as an injection outcome and in NIST AI 600-1 as a separate risk (Section 2.3), so merging security and content-safety harms is still a simplification. Source: GenAI-LLM-Top10 2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md; NIST AI 600-1 §2.3, §2.9.
- **P12-02** ("fundamentally challenging at the transformer level"). **Needs revision: from "overstated" to "partly".** OWASP LLM01:2026 now states LLMs make "no architectural distinction" between instructions and data and that no reliable prevention exists (R1-04, R1-05), and UK NCSC (Dec 2025) says the same (R1-43). These are expert positions by a standards body and a government agency, not a proof or architecture-level demonstration, so "documented as a widely held expert position, and every tested in-model defense has been broken adaptively (R1-21)" is fair; "proven impossible at transformer level" would still be overstated. Wording for the paper: "the model itself has no built-in boundary between instructions and data (OWASP 2026, NCSC 2025)".
- **P12-05** (defense in depth requires sanitizing inputs, e.g. Base64, role-play). **Holds: overstated.** OWASP LLM01:2026 lists input filtering (#3) as one of 11 controls and says filters are evadable by rephrasing or encoding (it names Base64 as a bypass, Common Example #8); the load-bearing controls are least privilege and capability limits. Nasr et al.: adaptive attacks above 90% against most of 12 defenses, with filters/detectors (Protect AI, PromptGuard, Model Armor) all above 90% (R1-20, R1-21). Note the "over 90%" is "most of 12", not all (PIGuard 71%).
- **P12-06** (OWASP Top 10 for LLMs as a tool; edition part only). **Holds: the 2026 edition is current**, released August 2026 (repo and Zenodo: August 4; resource page: August 3; announcement: September 1). Caveat for the paper: genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/ still shows 2025 as of October 15, 2026, so readers clicking that link will see the old list (R1-01, R1-15, R1-16). On garak, both phrases are the tool's own self-description (README title "LLM vulnerability scanner", subtitle "Red-teaming & Assessment Kit"; PyPI 0.17.0 summary "LLM vulnerability scanner"), which the other researcher may want (R1-27).
- **B2-01** (prompt-only alignment highly susceptible to jailbreaks). **Holds: confirmed indirectly.** Even trained-in safety is bypassed (Wei et al. 2023, R1-46); OWASP LLM01:2026 calls a constraining system prompt "a partial control only" that an attacker can bypass (mitigation #1), and says jailbreak prevention needs ongoing model training updates. No source measures "prompt-only alignment" as such, hence "indirectly".
- **B2-08** ("soft-prompt alignment (RLHF)"). **Holds: corrected.** Soft prompts are learned vectors with frozen model weights (Lester et al. 2021, R1-45); RLHF updates the weights; a written instruction like "do not be toxic" is a hard (discrete) prompt, neither of the two.

## Surprises

1. **The 2026 release date is August, not September 1.** The canonical repo, CITATION.cff and Zenodo say August 4, 2026; the OWASP resource page says August 3; September 1 is the announcement post. The project ledger's "released September 1" should become "published August 2026, announced September 1".
2. **The stale list page is just stale.** genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/ still shows 2025 (footer copyright 2025, newest item July 2025), and even the 2026 CITATION.cff points at it. Every OWASP primary (resource page, announcement, both repos) says 2026 is current. No OWASP statement explains the lag.
3. **The 2026 reordering matters for this part:** Excessive Agency rose from LLM06 to LLM03, and Improper Output Handling fell from LLM05 to LLM10. LLM01:2026 is far longer than 2025's and is itself agent-centred: it adopts the lethal trifecta and Meta's Rule of Two (calling the Rule of Two "a floor"), requires adaptive red-teaming, and names MCP tool-description poisoning.
4. **2025 to 2026 hardening of language:** "unclear if there are fool-proof methods" (2025) became "no reliable prevention mechanism exists today" (2026).
5. **Nasr et al.'s author list includes Anthropic, Google DeepMind and OpenAI-linked researchers**, so the "attacker moves second" result comes partly from labs that ship defenses. It is a preprint (v1 only).
6. **The Agent Control Standard is real but early**: version 0.1.3, a wire protocol for an external "Guardian" to allow, deny or modify agent actions. Its reference Guardian fails open by default. It has no effectiveness evidence yet.
7. **Vendor admissions differ.** OpenAI says outright that its measures reduce but don't remove the risk. Anthropic implies residual risk without stating it. Google's Workspace page claims its defenses have "consistently mitigated" attempts and admits no gaps.
8. **Nearly no independent measurement of system-level defenses.** CaMeL's 77% vs 84% figure is the authors' own. The one 2026 independent adaptive evaluation I found (Progent, Qwen2.5-7B) is small, and its authors say so.
