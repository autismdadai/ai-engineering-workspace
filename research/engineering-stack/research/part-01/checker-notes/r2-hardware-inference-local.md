## 5. Answers to the 13 questions (interim; PENDING items are marked)

**Q1. NVIDIA H100 datasheet.** NVIDIA's H100 product page (https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/, metadata date 2026-07-01) gives for H100 SXM: 80 GB memory, 3.35 TB/s, up to 700 W (configurable), NVLink 900 GB/s, PCIe Gen5 128 GB/s; Tensor Core rows TF32 989, BF16 1,979, FP16 1,979, FP8 3,958 teraFLOPS and INT8 3,958 TOPS, each with the footnote "With sparsity". So 3,958 is the sparse FP8 figure; the dense value is half, 1,979 (derived; Spheron prints 1,979; NVIDIA's 2022 launch blog Table 1 shows rounded 2,000 dense / 4,000 sparse). Dense FP16/BF16 is therefore about 989. The datasheet link on that page is https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-hopper-architecture/nvidia-tensor-core-gpu-datasheet (a landing page; the PDF behind it is password-protected per the fetch tool, so its own footnote wording was not read). Gap: the dense numbers are not printed in any NVIDIA document I could open.

**Q2. Hopper in-depth blog** (https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-hopper-architecture-in-depth/; published 2022-03-22, modified 2025-07-22). Full GH100: 144 SMs; H100 SXM5: 132; H100 PCIe: 114. FP8: E4M3 (4 exponent, 3 mantissa bits; "more precision") and E5M2 (5 exponent, 2 mantissa; "wider dynamic range"); the Transformer Engine, per layer, analyses output-tensor statistics and dynamically applies scaling factors. The fetch summary says the blog does not assign E4M3/E5M2 to weights/activations vs gradients. NVLink 4: 18 links, 900 GB/s total, "7x" PCIe Gen5 (128 GB/s total, 64 each way).

**Q3. "Unified memory pool"? What links nodes?** Not a fair description: see P4-08. DGX H100 guide: 640 GB total GPU memory (8 x 80 GB), no pooling language. Inside the node: NVLink 4 via NVSwitch, 900 GB/s per GPU. Between nodes: eight single-port ConnectX-7 NICs (InfiniBand default, up to 400 Gb/s each; Ethernet options 400GbE down to 10GbE), plus two dual-port ConnectX-7 for storage/in-band. The NVLink Switch System (up to 256 GPUs in one NVLink domain) was stated "not currently available with H100 systems" in the 2022 blog. Source: https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx/dgxh100-user-guide/introduction-to-dgxh100.html and the Hopper blog.

**Q4. Is H100 still current? NVIDIA's lineup.** Hopper is previous generation. Dated NVIDIA evidence opened: press release 2026-01-05 (Rubin in full production, partners H2 2026; Vera Rubin NVL72 and HGX Rubin NVL8). NVIDIA's H100 page navigation lists Vera Rubin, GB300 NVL72, GB200 NVL72. Not opened: a dated NVIDIA release for B200/GB200 or B300/GB300 shipping, and the Q2 FY27 earnings 8-K (Aug 2026). A web search for Rubin shipping status returned only 2025-era trade-press items (DCD, TweakTown, wccftech), not used. Gap: whether Rubin systems actually shipped by 2026-10-09.

**Q5. vLLM paper (arXiv 2309.06180).** Both: abstract and section 1 say "near-zero waste in KV cache memory"; no percentage in the text for what remains; waste is limited to within one block per request (section 4.3). Earlier systems: "only 20.4% - 38.2%" of KV-cache memory held actual token states (section 1, p. 2; section 3.1, p. 4 "as low as 20.4%"; Fig. 2 covers Orca Max/Pow2/Oracle versus vLLM, not FasterTransformer). vLLM bar in Fig. 2 is labelled 96.3 (inference: ~3.7% non-token-state). Throughput: 2-4x at the same latency versus FasterTransformer and Orca (abstract); section 6.2 on ShareGPT: 1.7-2.7x versus Orca (Oracle), 2.7-8x versus Orca (Max), up to 22x versus FasterTransformer; models OPT-13B (1 GPU), OPT-66B (4 GPUs), OPT-175B (8 GPUs), LLaMA-13B; A100 GPUs (40 GB for 13B, 80 GB for 175B). The advantage shrinks on OPT-175B with Alpaca. "Under 4%" comes from the vLLM announcement blog (2023-06-20, Woosuk Kwon et al., heading "The Secret Sauce: PagedAttention": "near-optimal memory usage, with a mere waste of under 4%"); the blog also says existing systems waste 60-80% of memory and reports up to 24x over HF Transformers and 3.5x over TGI (LLaMA-7B on A10G, LLaMA-13B on A100 40GB, ShareGPT lengths). Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.06180 ; https://vllm.ai/blog/2023-06-20-vllm. Caveat: both read through a fetch tool that summarises pages.

**Q6. Continuous batching origin.** Orca (Yu, Jeong, Kim, Kim, Chun; OSDI '22, July 2022, pp. 521-538) introduced iteration-level scheduling plus selective batching, reporting 36.9x throughput over NVIDIA FasterTransformer at the same latency on GPT-3 175B. The OSDI page does not contain the phrase "continuous batching"; vLLM's paper also uses "iteration-level scheduling" and does not use the phrase. Source: https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yu. Who coined "continuous batching" was not established here.

**Q7. Speculative decoding.** Leviathan, Kalman and Matias (ICML 2023; arXiv 2211.17192): Mp = target, Mq = approximation/draft, gamma = number of guesses per iteration; acceptance rule and resampling distribution norm(max(0, p - q)) as in P10-06; identical-distribution guarantee (Appendix A.1, section 6); expected tokens per iteration (1 - alpha^(gamma+1)) / (1 - alpha) (Eq. 1, section 3.1). Reported speedups: T5-XXL (11B) target with T5-small (77M), T5-base (250M), T5-large (800M) approximators on one TPU-v4, batch size 1, versus T5X: English-German translation 3.4x (temp 0) and 2.6x (temp 1) with T5-small; CNN/DM summarisation 3.1x (temp 0) and 2.3x (temp 1) with T5-small; abstract says 2X-3X. Chen et al. (DeepMind, arXiv 2302.01318, submitted 2023-02-02): speculative sampling on Chinchilla 70B, 2-2.5x decoding speedup in a distributed setup; draft size not stated on the abstract page. Sources: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/leviathan23a/leviathan23a.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318.

**Q8. Where could "1B draft, 70B target doubles tokens/s" come from?** NVIDIA TensorRT-LLM blog (2024-12-17; Shah, Eassa, Putterman, Vaidya, Gangani, Srivastava): Llama 3.3 70B target, one H200 ("DGX H200, TP1"), FP8 weights and KV cache, batch size 1, draft length 10: no draft 51.14 tokens/s; Llama 3.2 1B draft 181.74 (3.55x); 3B draft 161.53 (3.16x); Llama 3.1 8B draft 134.38 (2.63x). vLLM blog (2024-10-17): Llama3-70B on 4x H100; draft model turboderp/Qwama-0.5B-Instruct up to 1.5x on ShareGPT at QPS=1; n-gram up to 2.8x on CNN/DailyMail at QPS=1; slowdowns 1.4x (ShareGPT) and 1.8x (CNN/DM) at high QPS; batch sizes not given. AMD and papers: not searched (budget). The "doubles" figure is within the range for low-batch use and wrong as a general rule. Sources: https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/boost-llama-3-3-70b-inference-throughput-3x-with-nvidia-tensorrt-llm-speculative-decoding/ ; https://vllm.ai/blog/2024-10-17-spec-decode.

**Q9. Memory-bandwidth-bound decoding (primary source).** vLLM paper section 1 (p. 1) and section 2.2 (p. 3); Leviathan section 1 (p. 1), which also says speculative decoding fits when compute is spare. Caveat in vLLM section 6.2: large models on short-output workloads can become compute-bound.
