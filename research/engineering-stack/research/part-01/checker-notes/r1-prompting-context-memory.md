### Q1. Induction heads (Olsson et al. 2022)

Short answer: a Transformer Circuits Thread article plus an arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed at a venue. The authors claim strong, causal-type evidence only for small attention-only models and correlational evidence for larger models. The abrupt change comes early in training and PCA is used. Later studies are split on large models.

- Venue: W3 is dated Mar 8, 2022, sits under "Transformer Circuits Thread", names no venue and makes no peer-review statement; its comments section says external researchers were invited to comment (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html). The arXiv page shows v1 only (2022-09-24), no comments, no journal reference, no DOI (https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895). I found no later venue for it on the pages I opened.
- Authors' strength statement (W3 Introduction and Arguments 3 and 6): strong evidence that attention heads are the mechanism for small attention-only models (ablations, mechanistic analysis); for larger models with MLPs the evidence is mainly correlational and could be confounded; no ablations for full-scale models; extrapolating across orders of magnitude needs caution; other composition mechanisms may also form during the phase change; the work is the beginnings of evidence. The arXiv abstract (via W2/W4) calls the evidence preliminary and indirect and says induction heads "may" account for most in-context learning in large models.
- Timing: in-context learning develops abruptly in a narrow window early in training, roughly 2.5 to 5 billion tokens; the score goes from under 0.15 nats before to about 0.4 after; the loss curve has a bump; for large models the window is maybe 1 to 2 percent of the way through training (W3 Argument 1).
- PCA: yes. It is applied to per-token loss vectors (log-likelihoods on 10,000 random tokens across model snapshots); the first two components show the training trajectories pivoting in the same window (W3). This is output (loss) space, not weight space.
- W1 (a blog) says the large-model evidence rests on correlation across 34 models of 13M to 13B parameters; I could not verify that figure in the part of W3 I read.
- Later studies (abstract pages only; venues and full texts not opened):
  - Yin & Steinhardt, arXiv 2502.14010v1 (2025-02-19): 12 language models (parameter range not on the abstract page). Few-shot in-context learning depends mainly on function-vector (FV) heads, more so in larger models; many FV heads start as induction heads during training and later transition to the FV mechanism.
  - Crosbie & Shutova, arXiv 2407.07011v3 (2025-04-02): Llama-3-8B and InternLM2-20B (sizes from the model names). Ablating a small number of induction heads cut accuracy on abstract pattern tasks by up to about 32 percent (near random) and brought the few-shot gain on NLP tasks close to zero-shot level; attention knockout gave finer-grained evidence. Number of heads ablated: not on the page.
  - Singh et al., arXiv 2404.07129v1 (2024-04-10): controlled synthetic-data setting, model sizes not on the abstract page. Induction heads appear abruptly with a phase change in the loss; three interacting subcircuits, found by clamping activations during training, drive formation; induction heads are diverse and additive. It does not test large models.
  - Reddy, arXiv 2312.03002v1 (2023-12-03): a minimal attention-only network on a simplified synthetic in-context classification task. In-context learning arises from the abrupt emergence of an induction head that competes with in-weights learning; a two-parameter induction head model matches the data dependence; the abruptness is attributed to learning three nested logits in sequence.
- Inference: the abrupt-formation story is well supported in small, synthetic or attention-only settings. For large models, ablations support a role for induction heads in pattern-matching tasks, but Yin & Steinhardt argue FV heads, not induction heads, mainly drive few-shot learning, especially at larger scale. So "explains few-shot learning" and "causal reality" overstate it.
- Gaps: parameter ranges for Yin & Steinhardt, Singh and Reddy (full texts not opened); venues for all four; anything published after these studies; the last ~24,000 characters of W3.

### Q2. Lost in the middle (Liu et al.)

Short answer: in 2023-era models on two tasks, accuracy was highest when the relevant item was at the start or end and lowest in the middle. In the 2024 to 2025 studies I opened, length degradation is robust, but the U-shaped position effect is not reproduced in Chroma's 2025 needle test.

- Liu et al. (arXiv 2307.03172v3, stamp 2023-11-20; TACL 2024 per the brief, not shown on page 1 of the PDF). Tasks: multi-document QA (NaturalQuestions-Open passages, exactly one with the answer; number of documents and answer position varied) and key-value retrieval (JSON of random UUID pairs). Models: GPT-3.5-Turbo (0613, 4K) and its 16K version, Claude-1.3 (8K) and Claude-1.3 (100K), MPT-30B-Instruct (8,192 tokens), LongChat-13B (16K); GPT-4 (8K) on a 500-example subset (Appendix D); also Flan-T5-XXL and Flan-UL2 (Section 4.1), base MPT-30B (Section 4.3), Llama-2 7B, 13B, 70B with and without chat tuning (Appendix E).
- Findings: a U-shaped curve (Section 2.3). With 20 or 30 documents, GPT-3.5-Turbo with the answer mid-context scored below its closed-book 56.1 percent (Section 2.3). On key-value retrieval, GPT-3.5-Turbo (both lengths) and MPT-30B-Instruct struggled most in the middle, Claude-1.3 was near perfect, and LongChat-13B (16K) showed a different pattern at 140 pairs (Section 3.2). Extended-context versions performed almost the same as shorter ones when the input fit both. Llama-2 13B and 70B show the U-shape; 7B shows only recency bias (Appendix E). Advice: rerank so relevant passages come first, truncate ranked lists, and repeat the query before and after data (helps key-value retrieval, barely changes multi-document QA) (Sections 4.2 and 5).
- Follow-ups I opened:
  - RULER, arXiv 2404.06654v3 (2024-08-06): 17 models, 13 tasks; near-perfect on vanilla needle-in-a-haystack, large drops as length grows; only about half of models claiming 32K or more keep satisfactory performance at 32K; Yi-34B (200K claimed) singled out. The abstract page does not report position effects.
  - NoLiMa, arXiv 2502.05167v3 (2025-07-09; ICML 2025 per arXiv comments): 13 models claiming at least 128K tokens; at 32K, 11 fall below 50 percent of their short-context baseline; GPT-4o drops from 99.3 to 69.7 percent (29.6 percentage points); needles share minimal wording with the question; reasoning-enhanced models and chain-of-thought did not fully prevent the decline.
  - Chroma "Context Rot" (2025-07-14; company report, not peer-reviewed): 18 models (Claude Opus 4, Sonnet 4, Sonnet 3.7, Sonnet 3.5, Haiku 3.5; o3, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini and nano, GPT-4o, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-3.5 Turbo; Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash, 2.0 Flash; Qwen3-235B-A22B, 32B, 8B). Over 11 needle positions it found no notable variation in its needle task, so no U-shape there; performance degraded with input length in every experiment; early placement was more accurate on the repeated-words task. Failure styles differ: Claude models showed the lowest hallucination rates under distractors and tended to abstain; GPT models the highest hallucination rates; Gemini 2.5 produced random words from about 500 to 750 words in the repeated-words task; shuffled haystacks beat coherent ones across all 18 models.
- Which models still show the effect: in what I opened, only the 2023-era models of Liu et al. are shown with the U-shape; no opened study shows it for 2025 frontier models, and Chroma saw none in its needle task. Which show less of it: not established. I have no per-model position comparison; Chroma separates failure style (abstain versus hallucinate), and NoLiMa names GPT-4o among the better performers, yet it still fell 29.6 percentage points at 32K.
- Inference: position effects depend on model, task and how full the window is; length degradation has not gone away.
- Gaps: vendor long-context reports and other 2025 to 2026 position studies were not reached (budget); a 2025 paper on how position biases shift near the window limit (Veseli et al., title from my recollection) was not opened.

### Q3. SGLang and RadixAttention

- Latest arXiv version: v2, dated 2024-06-06, title "SGLang: Efficient Execution of Structured Language Model Programs"; the v1 title was "Efficiently Programming Large Language Models using SGLang". The arXiv page names no venue (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07104). Peer-reviewed venue: could not check (budget); the OpenReview page was blocked, and NeurIPS 2024 is only my background expectation.
- Confirmed in v2 (https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07104v2, Section 3): a radix tree maps token sequences to KV tensors; reuse across generation calls is automatic; LRU eviction takes the least recently used leaf first; each node keeps a reference counter of running requests and is evictable only at zero. v1 also has LRU, reference counting and cache-aware scheduling that sorts requests by matched prefix length (Sections 5.1 and 5.2).
- Depth-first search is about scheduling order, not prefix matching: Theorem 3.1 says that in the offline case with cache at least as large as the longest request, visiting the request radix tree in DFS order gives the optimal cache hit rate, and longest-shared-prefix-first is equivalent to DFS order; online arrivals disrupt the order. Matching itself is a radix-tree lookup (Appendix A.2, Algorithm 1). I did not see the proof (Appendix A.3).
- Throughput with scope: the v2 abstract says up to 6.4x higher throughput than state-of-the-art inference systems across agent control, reasoning, few-shot benchmarks, JSON decoding, RAG pipelines and multi-turn chat. Section 6.2 repeats up to 6.4x throughput and up to 3.7x lower latency on open-weight models, mainly from Llama-7B charts. Setup: mostly AWS EC2 G5 (NVIDIA A10G 24GB), some A100 80GB; models Llama-2 7B to 70B, Mixtral-8x7B, LLaVA-v1.5-7B, LLaVA-NeXT-34B, GPT-3.5 via API; baselines Guidance v0.1.8, vLLM v0.2.5, LMQL v0.7.3. Multi-modal (Table 2): LLaVA-v1.5-7B 1.15 versus 0.18 images per second; LLaVA-NeXT-34B 0.10 versus 0.02 frames per second. v1 abstract: up to 5x speed-up, metric unnamed. Chatbot Arena deployment over one month: cache hit rates 52.4 and 74.1 percent.
- Inference: the baselines are early-2024 versions, so the speed-ups do not transfer to current vLLM. No "thousands of parallel requests" claim was found (P2-14).
- Gaps: W6's evaluation section was not captured; the published-proceedings page was not opened.

### Q4. Quadratic attention and KV cache growth

- Vaswani et al. 2017 (arXiv 1706.03762), Section 4 "Why Self-Attention", Table 1 on p. 6: self-attention per-layer complexity O(n^2 d), O(1) sequential operations, O(1) maximum path length; recurrent O(n d^2), O(n), O(n); convolutional O(k n d^2), O(1), O(log_k n); restricted self-attention O(r n d), O(1), O(n/r); n is sequence length, d is representation dimension. The paper does not discuss a KV cache or decoding memory (per fetch).
- KV cache: vLLM paper (SOSP '23, arXiv 2309.06180), Section 3: OPT-13B needs about 800 KB per token (2 x 5120 x 40 x 2 bytes, FP16) and up to about 1.6 GB for a 2048-token sequence; Section 1: the cache grows and shrinks as tokens are generated; on an A100 40GB serving a 13B model about 30 percent of memory held KV states. The paper does not say "linear" in words (per fetch); the constant per token implies it.

### Q5. Is "context engineering inherently involves manipulating the KV cache" accurate?

Short answer: no. It conflates three layers.
- Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents" (2025-09-29): defines context as the tokens included when sampling; context engineering curates the best set of tokens at inference, the "natural progression" of prompt engineering. Covers system prompts, tools, examples, just-in-time retrieval, compaction, structured note-taking, sub-agents and MCP; discusses an attention budget, n^2 pairwise relationships and context rot; treats context as a finite resource with diminishing returns. It never mentions the KV cache or prompt caching (per fetch).
- Provider prompt caching (Anthropic docs): the developer places cache breakpoints (automatic or explicit); the prefix order is tools, system, messages; hits need 100 percent identical segments; default 5-minute lifetime, 1-hour option at 2x price; reads cost 0.1x base input on most models; the docs mention KV cache representations only under data retention (held in memory, not stored at rest) and say caching does not change outputs.
- Server-side KV reuse: RadixAttention in SGLang reuses KV tensors automatically across requests inside an engine the operator runs (Q3).
- Inference: application-level curation decides what tokens go in; keeping a stable prefix helps cache hits, but manipulating KV tensors is not part of the practice for hosted-API users.

### Q6. Role prompting, determinism and Structured Outputs

- Does any source support "role prompting enforces determinism"? None in anything I opened, and I ran no dedicated search (budget).
- OpenAI Structured Outputs guide (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs): guarantees that responses adhere to the supplied JSON Schema; JSON mode only guarantees valid JSON; refusals are programmatically detectable. It makes no claim that values are correct and does not discuss determinism. So the guarantee is schema conformance, not content.
- Thinking Machines (2025-09-10): temperature-0 output is not deterministic in practice because kernels lack batch invariance and server load changes batch size; atomic adds are not the main cause. Test: Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, prompt "Tell me about Richard Feynman.", 1,000 completions of 1,000 tokens at temperature 0: 80 unique outputs with default kernels (most common one 78 times; first divergence at token 103), 1 unique output with batch-invariant kernels. The fetched summary reports open-weight tests only; hosted APIs were not covered.
