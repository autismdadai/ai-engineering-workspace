## (c) Changes the picture

1. Hopper is no longer the centre. NVIDIA says HGX B200 and B300 are "shipping now" and GB300 NVL72 is "Available Now". Vera Rubin is "ramping into full production" (Aug 26, 2026 results) and its pages also say "Available Now". MLPerf Inference v6.1 has zero H100 results, and its Rubin results were "preview" (H031, H032, H048, H051, H056, H057). AMD's MI355X (288 GB HBM3E, 8 TB/s) matches Blackwell Ultra's memory and ran the round's largest system: 512 GPUs (H034, H058).
2. NVIDIA's own pages disagree on Rubin per-GPU numbers: 22 TB/s and 3.6 TB/s NVLink (HGX page) versus 19.2 TB/s and 3 TB/s (NVL72 page, footnoted "at-scale AI factory"). The HGX NVL8 board total, 130 TB/s, works out to 16.25 TB/s per GPU. Cite page and date, and prefer rack totals (H053, H055).
3. NVIDIA spec tables mix sparse and dense numbers. H100 and H200 FP8 (3,958) are "with sparsity". GB300 NVL72 is "with sparsity unless otherwise noted". The HGX Blackwell table says "Dense is ½ sparse". Rubin's NVFP4 inference figure is sparse while its training figures are dense. Every compute number in the paper needs a sparse or dense label (H042, H050, H052, H053, H055, H057).
4. NVIDIA's 2022 "Hopper Architecture In-Depth" table is preliminary: 3,000 GB/s and FP8 2,000/4,000, against the shipping 3.35 TB/s and 3,958 with sparsity. Use it for SM counts and NVLink design, not for shipping specs (H041, H042).
5. Transformer Engine's defaults depend on the GPU. Hopper and Ada get delayed scaling with HYBRID (E4M3 forward, E5M2 backward). Blackwell 10.x gets MXFP8: E4M3 for all tensors, with one E8M0 scale per 32 values. Compute capability 12.x gets current scaling. NVFP4 is available from 10.0. "Dynamic scaling" is not TE's term (H002, H004, H006, H007).
6. Inside a DGX H100: local HBM at 3.35 TB/s is about 7.4x faster than NVLink's 450 GB/s per direction, and NVLink per direction is 9x one 400 Gb/s ConnectX-7 (50 GB/s). NVIDIA lists 640 GB as 8 x 80 GB, not one pool (H036, H038, H043; arithmetic).
7. Multi-node training is commonly compute-heavy when communication is overlapped. NVIDIA reports 41 to 48% MFU on up to 6,144 H100s, falling from 47% to 42% when strong-scaling from 96 to 4,608 GPUs. These figures are self-reported and the precision is not stated (H026).
