## (a) The 10 facts the writer most needs

1. **Two famous comparison sources are frozen.** Hugging Face's Open LLM Leaderboard retired on March 13, 2025 after "over 13K models" (C023, C024), and HELM entered maintenance mode on June 1, 2026, with "no new evaluations" added to its leaderboards (C005). Older model cards still carry Open LLM Leaderboard numbers (C011).
2. **Arena (formerly Chatbot Arena, then LMArena) ranks by crowd votes**: anonymous pairs, Bradley-Terry scores with confidence intervals (ICML 2024, C012, C013), with "style control" for length and markdown (C019). Its own paper says its users and prompts may not match production or specialist use (C014). Its links now resolve to arena.ai under the name "Arena" (C020, inferred from redirects).
3. **The Leaderboard Illusion dispute is mostly about policy, not arithmetic.** Singh et al. (preprint) say private multi-variant testing, unequal sampling and data access distort Arena (C015); Arena's policy allows exactly that testing and data sharing (C017, C018), and its reply disputes several numbers, for example that the 112% gain was measured on Arena-Hard, not Arena (C016).
4. **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2** averages 10 evaluations it runs itself, weighted toward agentic tasks (agents 30%, general 30%, coding 20%, scientific reasoning 20%), uses private datasets and LLM grading, and says it "may not apply directly to every use case" (C021, C022).
5. **LiveBench** limits contamination with fresh, objectively scored questions, yet its own changelog reports contaminated and saturated tasks; its newest changelog entry was 284 days old on October 19, 2026 (C001 to C003).
6. **Contamination is measured, not hypothetical:** GSM1k found drops of "up to 8%" from GSM8k with some families overfitting across sizes (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks, C025); Deng et al. found GPT-4 could fill masked MMLU options 57% of the time (NAACL 2024, C026).
7. **Licences differ inside one family, so read each card.** Meta: Llama 4 Community License, Muse Glimmer 30B Apache-2.0, Muse Spark Open a custom gated "Muse Model License" (C038, C041 to C043). Qwen3.8-27B is Apache-2.0 but the 2.4T flagship is "qwen3.8-max" (C044, C047). Mistral spans Apache 2.0, Modified MIT and CC BY-NC 4.0, and two of its own docs pages contradict each other (C039, C046).
8. **Newest releases seen (publisher pages, October 19, 2026):** Muse Spark Open 816B/42B active, October 15, 2026 (C042); DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, MIT, about October 16 to 17 (C045); Qwen3.8, August 2026 (C033); Granite 4.1 3B/8B/30B, Apache 2.0, 512K (C035); Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B, MIT, March 4, 2026, 16K context (C048); gpt-oss-120b and 20b, Apache 2.0 (C034); Gemma 4, Apache 2.0 (Part 11, C049).
9. **What to read on a model card:** Mitchell et al.'s nine sections (C029, C030; FAT* '19 per the authors' PDF, C031) and Hugging Face's metadata (`license`, `base_model`, `datasets`) and template (C007 to C009). Cards mostly report aggregate, self-run benchmarks (C009); Hugging Face now marks scores "verified" only when run in HF Jobs with inspect-ai (C010). Checklist: C032.
10. **Every kind of source says to test on your own task:** OpenAI ("experiment with different models", C050), Anthropic ("having a good evaluation set is the most important step", C051), Mistral (C052), Meta, Microsoft and Artificial Analysis (C053). Open weights do not mean small: Muse Spark Open needs at least 700 GB of GPU memory at NVFP4 (C054).
