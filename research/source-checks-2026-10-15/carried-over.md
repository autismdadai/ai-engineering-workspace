## Program-of-Thoughts and PAL (Section 9.5, line 1128)

- carried-C05 MISSING_SCOPE. The manual's quote is faithful: arXiv 2211.12588 abstract says PoT "uses language models (mainly Codex) to express the reasoning process as a program", reports "an average performance gain over CoT of around 12% across all datasets" (abstract wording varies slightly between the abs listing and the HTML), and evaluates "five math word problem datasets" and "three financial-QA datasets". Comments: "Published at TMLR 2023." But the paper's own body ties the 12% to a narrower setting: the HTML Introduction reads "Under the zero-shot setting, the average gain over CoT is around 12% for the MWP datasets", and gives few-shot averages of about 8% (math word problems) and about 15% (financial QA) (those two few-shot figures were read off by the fetch tool, not quoted). The paper never says points or relative; Tables 2 and 3 arithmetic suggests points (about 12.2 and 12.4, my reading). The main backend is Codex (code-davinci-002); the zero-shot CoT baselines come from Kojima et al. (GPT-3). Fix: state "around 12% on average (abstract); about 12% zero-shot on the math datasets, about 8% and 15% few-shot on math and financial sets", mainly Codex.
- carried-C06 VERIFIED. PAL, arXiv 2211.10435: abstract says PAL with Codex reaches state-of-the-art few-shot accuracy on GSM8K, "surpassing PaLM-540B which uses chain-of-thought by absolute 15% top-1"; HTML Table 1 GSM8K column: CoT PaLM-540b 56.9, PAL (Codex) 72.0 (gap 15.1 points; cell values read off a flattened table). Backend "Codex (code-davinci-002)", greedy decoding at temperature 0. Manual's "about 15 percentage points top-1 (2022 models)" matches. Scope detail worth one clause: the 56.9 is taken from Wei et al. (2022), not re-run by the PAL authors, and the PAL figure is a three-run average. ICML 2023 confirmed earlier (PMLR 202:10764-10799).

## Reflexion (Section 11.3, line 1668)

- carried-C08 VERIFIED. arXiv 2303.11366 (v4) abstract: "Reflexion achieves a 91% pass@1 accuracy on the HumanEval coding benchmark, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GPT-4 that achieves 80%." HTML Table 1, HumanEval (PY) row: Prev SOTA "65.8 (CodeT + GPT-3.5)", SOTA "80.1 (GPT-4)", Reflexion "91.0". The manual's "reported against a GPT-4 baseline of 80% cited in the paper" is accurate.
- carried-C09 MISSING_SCOPE. Three scope points the manual leaves out. (1) The self-reflection loop for HumanEval uses unit tests the model writes itself ("To generate a test suite, we use Chain-of-Thought prompting"; filtered to up to 6 tests), not the benchmark's hidden tests; the paper says this is why the result is "eligible for pass@1 accuracy reporting". So the Caveat sentence ("external success signal... ground-truth signal") needs care: for code the signal is running self-written tests, which is executable feedback but not ground truth. (2) The paper itself notes "the inferior performance of Reflexion to the baseline GPT-4 on MBPP Python". (3) The HTML text I could read names GPT-4 as the base model only in the Table 3 caption (HumanEval Rust ablation); the base-model attribution for the 91.0 Python figure is not stated in the tables or text I could read, so the manual's "With GPT-4 as the base model" is likely but not confirmed from this source.

## DSPy (Section 12.4, line 1958; Chapter 28 item 5)

- carried-C13 VERIFIED (quotes). arXiv 2310.03714 abstract: "self-bootstrap pipelines that outperform standard few-shot prompting (generally by over 25% and 65%, respectively)" and "pipelines with expert-created demonstrations (by up to 5-46% and 16-40%, respectively)", with GPT-3.5 and llama2-13b-chat in that order; "We conduct two case studies". arXiv page shows one version, v1, 5 Oct 2023.
- carried-C14 MISSING_SCOPE. The paper never says whether "25%" and "65%" are relative gains or percentage points, and the body text does not restate them. From arXiv v1 HTML tables (read through a fetch tool, so treat cell values as read-off, not quoted): GSM8K, GPT-3.5 dev, few-shot 33.1 vs bootstrap x2 64.7 (about +31.6 points); llama2-13b-chat dev, few-shot 4.3 vs bootstrap x2 37.3 (+33.0 points, about 8.7x relative); HotPotQA, GPT-3.5 dev answer EM, few-shot 34.3 vs multihop bootstrap 48.7 (+14.4 points, +42% relative). So "over 25%" and "65%" cannot be points in every case; they fit relative gains better. Fix: write "reported gains of over 25% (GPT-3.5) and 65% (llama2-13b-chat) over few-shot prompting in two 2023 case studies (GSM8K and HotPotQA); the paper does not state whether these are relative or points", and keep the existing 2023-models caveat.
